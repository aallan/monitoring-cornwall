About

Monitoring Cornwall shows which of the county's rivers are high and rising, which storm overflows are spilling, how clean the water is at each beach, and what the rain and the rivers are forecast to do next. All of it is public data, published by the Environment Agency, South West Water, the Met Office and others on their own sites; here it's on one map, and most of it is checked every five minutes.

On the map

Flood warnings
The Environment Agency's flood warnings and alerts in force in Cornwall, outlined on the map, with a banner across the top of the page while any are in force.
Rivers and rain
About 130 river level, tidal and rain gauges, reporting every 15 minutes. River gauges are green within their typical range and red above it, with an arrow while the level is rising or falling. Rain gauges have a 48-hour rain forecast, and the gauges on the Tamar, Lynher, Fowey and Camel a fortnight's river flow forecast.
Storm overflows
Nearly 400 storm overflows run by South West Water in Cornwall, which release storm sewage into rivers and the sea when heavy rain overloads the sewers. The map opens on those spilling now, and each one's popup shows its spills over the last 30 days and its yearly totals.
Coast
Cornwall's 90 designated bathing waters, coloured by their latest annual classification, with recent sample results and, from May to September, the Environment Agency's daily pollution risk forecast. Wave buoys and weather stations around the coast, from Bude to St Mary's Sound, and two more offshore, the Seven Stones lightvessel and the E1 buoy, report waves, wind and sea temperature between them.
Background
Rivers, streams, railways and the A30, A38 and A39, from OpenStreetMap. Rivers end at the county boundary rather than running out to sea down the middle of each estuary, and heritage railways aren't shown.
